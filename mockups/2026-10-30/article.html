A quieter corner of the internet.

I miss an internet that felt like people talking to people. Maybe we can make a little room for it again.

written byRuss HeritageCulture & the internet6 min read

Somewhere along the way, being online stopped feeling like going somewhere. I used to open a browser and visit places. A friend's journal. A photography forum. Somebody's gloriously impractical little website. They felt different because the people who made them were different.

There were no recommendations waiting to steer me away. No one was asking me to build a personal brand. Mostly, we were just making things and hoping somebody would find them interesting.

I don't think I miss the technology all that much. I miss the feeling that there was a person on the other end of it.

A smaller kind of internet

I'm not pretending the old internet was perfect. Anyone who ever had to wait for a photograph to load one line at a time knows better. But there was something about its rough edges that left room for us.

A website could look a little strange. A journal entry could be about absolutely nothing. You didn't need a strategy to share a photograph you liked, or a thought you hadn't quite finished thinking.

Maybe that's what I want more of now. Things made at the scale of a person. A few paragraphs that sound like somebody talking. A picture that asks you to look instead of asking you to keep scrolling.

I want to make a place that feels like someone lives here.

Leaving the door open

That's part of why I'm making this space. The photographs and the words come from the same person, even when they seem to be about different things. A portrait is one way of paying attention. Writing is another.

Some days I'll have something to say. Other days, I'll have a photograph, a question, or a small thing I noticed on the way home. I'd like there to be room for all of that here.

A personal website doesn't have to be a performance. It can be a room with the door open. You can stay for a while, look around, and come back when you feel like it.

A place to begin

I can't make the whole internet feel like that again. But I can make this little part of it feel a little more like me. And I can keep looking for the other people doing the same.

That seems like a good enough place to start.
